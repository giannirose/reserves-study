Method Notes

This reserve study generally follows the component-based reserve study framework established by national industry guidance — commonly referenced as CAI (Community Associations Institute) and APRA (Association of Professional Reserve Analysts) standards. In practical terms, that means: identifying major shared components, estimating their replacement cost and expected life, projecting when each will need replacement, and comparing the association's reserve fund to a "Fully Funded Balance" benchmark — the reserve level the component method calls for at any given point.

A few deliberate variations from strict industry-standard methodology are used in this study, chosen to better fit this association's practical funding priorities. Each is explained below so the reasoning is clear to anyone reviewing this study.

Where This Study Intentionally Varies

The contribution amount is set directly, not solved for

The annual contribution shown throughout this study is set as a planning assumption (with a planned annual increase), rather than mathematically calculated to hit a specific funding target. The "Percent Funded" figure reported each year is a diagnostic measurement — how the chosen contribution compares to the standard fully-funded benchmark — not an automatically self-correcting number. Adjusting the contribution to change that trajectory is a decision for the association's board.

Funding for long-lived components is deferred until it's relevant

This study covers a 30-year planning horizon. Under strict component-method accounting, a component with a longer expected life — say, 50 years — would begin accruing funding from the day it's installed, even though its replacement is decades away. This study instead defers funding for such components until their replacement falls within the same 30-year horizon used for every other component. This avoids asking current contributions to carry the weight of far-future replacements while nearer-term components compete for the same dollars.

Inflation rate — global default with optional per-component override

Each component’s current replacement cost is entered in today’s dollars and compounded forward at an annual inflation rate to the year replacement is expected — so a component scheduled ten years out is shown at its estimated cost in that future year, not at today’s price.

A global inflation rate is set in Settings and applied to all components by default. Where real-world cost escalation for a specific component is known to differ materially from the general rate — construction costs, roofing, and paving are common examples — an individual inflation rate can be entered in the Components tab for that component. When a per-component rate is set, it overrides the global rate for that component’s cost projections throughout the study: current cost, expenditures, and fully funded balance. Components without a per-component rate continue to use the global rate.

Interest earned is an estimate, not a guarantee

Annual interest is estimated using an average-balance approximation. Rather than applying the assumed 2.0% rate to the opening balance alone, this study applies it to a balance adjusted for the contributions and expenditures expected to occur during the year — specifically, the opening balance plus half the year's net inflows — on the premise that cash movements happen throughout the year rather than all at once. This produces a modestly more conservative interest estimate in years with large expenditures, and a modestly higher estimate in years with strong contributions.

The simpler and perhaps more common approach applies the interest rate to the opening balance only and treats contributions and expenditures as occurring at year-end. That method is slightly easier to audit against a single balance figure, but it does not reflect the practical reality that reserves earn interest on a balance that is changing throughout the year. The difference between the two methods is generally small; it becomes more noticeable in years with unusually large expenditures scheduled early in the year.

Regardless of method, actual interest earned will depend on real account activity, prevailing rates, and the timing of actual cash movements, and should be reviewed periodically against actual statements.

Special assessments and loans are not automatically modeled

This study tracks whether the association's governing documents permit special assessments or loans, but does not automatically incorporate either into the projected cash flow. If a projected shortfall appears in this study, it is a signal for the board to evaluate available options directly — including, but not limited to, a special assessment, a loan, or an adjustment to the regular contribution.

Questions or Further Review

This study is intended as a practical planning tool for the association's board. For decisions with binding legal or financial consequences, the board should consult a professional reserve specialist or financial advisor familiar with this association's specific circumstances.
